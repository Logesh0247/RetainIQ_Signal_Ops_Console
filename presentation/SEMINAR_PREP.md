# RetainIQ seminar prep

**Deck:** 22 slides · **planned speaking time:** 11:55, before questions  
**Core message:** RetainIQ turns historical churn data into customer-level risk scores, understandable model signals, and rule-based retention actions. It is a decision-support prototype—not proof that a campaign saves revenue.

## The story to remember

1. **Problem:** teams learn who left after the fact; blanket offers waste effort.
2. **Signal:** clean and encode the customer records; remove post-outcome leakage.
3. **Model:** compare four algorithms, select Logistic Regression, measure its limits.
4. **Action:** prioritize risk, show model contributors, recommend rule-based actions.
5. **Honesty:** results are internal to one historical telecom dataset; campaign uplift, external validation, subgroup fairness, and live monitoring remain future work.

A useful one-sentence explanation: **“Power BI shows portfolio patterns; RetainIQ scores uploaded customer rows and helps an operator decide whom to review and what action to consider.”**

## Important accuracy guardrails

These are the points most likely to be probed. The slide wording and speaker notes have been adjusted to avoid overstating them; keep the same precision when speaking.

| Topic | Safe seminar wording |
|---|---|
| **Test split / selection** | The 1,409-row test split was held out from model fitting, but it was used to compare and select candidate models. Treat the reported test metrics as **internal evaluation**, not a final untouched or external validation. A separate cohort or nested evaluation is a next step. |
| **Risk-band rates** | The 9.5% / 42.0% / 73.0% observed churn rates are calculated across the full 7,043-row dataset. They show in-dataset ordering; they are **not independent validation** and should not be presented as a deployment guarantee. |
| **Fairness** | Senior-customer churn is 41.7% vs 23.6% for non-seniors (about 1.77×); overall churn is 26.54%. This is a group-rate difference, **not proof of model bias**. The model includes senior status, but subgroup error rates and offer allocation have not been audited. |
| **Revenue** | $92,539 high-risk and $200,302 high-plus-medium are sums of current monthly charges in those groups: **exposure at risk**, not expected savings, recovered revenue, or measured campaign uplift. |
| **Thresholds** | The deployed binary label uses a 0.50 cutoff. The 0.35 threshold and class-weight experiment are measured alternatives, not the deployed setting. Changing the threshold is a business decision about capacity and the cost of false alarms. |
| **Risk tiers vs binary label** | Risk bands are separate: low <30%, medium 30–<60%, high ≥60%. So a 55% score can have the binary label “likely to churn” at 0.50 and still be in the medium band. That is intentional. |
| **Recommendations** | Recommendations are deterministic rules based on customer fields and risk—not generated by the ML model. Their campaign effectiveness has not been A/B tested. |
| **Explanations** | Production drivers are Logistic Regression coefficient × encoded feature value. Describe them as model contributors/associations, not causes. SHAP was explored in research only. |
| **Power BI relationship** | Present Power BI as the portfolio-level descriptive layer and RetainIQ as uploaded-row scoring/operations. Do not imply a live synchronized BI-to-app feed unless you demonstrate one. |
| **Roadmap** | The 14 phases describe the **core project workflow**. Comparison, technology, responsible-AI, demo, and thank-you slides frame or close that workflow. |

## Numbers worth memorizing

### Data and preprocessing

- 7,043 customers: 1,869 churned (26.54%), 5,174 retained (73.46%).
- 34 raw fields → 30 model features; three numeric + 27 binary flags.
- 5,634 train / 1,409 test rows. Split is stratified; rates round to 26.54% in both.
- 11 blank `TotalCharges` values, all zero-tenure customers.
- Leakage fields removed: Churn Score, Churn Reason, Churn Category, Customer Status.

### Logistic Regression at the deployed 0.50 cutoff

| Metric | Result | Plain-language interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Accuracy | 80.34% | Overall correct classifications; compare with 73.46% majority baseline. |
| Precision | 64.74% | Of customers flagged as churners, about 65% were churners. |
| Recall | 56.95% | Caught 213 of 374 churners; 161 were missed. |
| F1 | 60.60% | Balance of precision and recall. |
| ROC-AUC | 84.91% | Ranking quality across thresholds, not “84.91% accurate.” |

Confusion matrix: **TN 919 · FP 116 · FN 161 · TP 213**. Five-fold CV on training data: F1 **62.11% ± 2.96%**; accuracy **81.19% ± 1.18%**.

### Operations and value

- Risk bands: **4,435 low · 1,473 medium · 1,135 high**; high + medium = **2,608**.
- In-dataset observed churn by band: **9.5% · 42.0% · 73.0%**.
- Monthly-charge exposure: **$92,539 high · $200,302 high + medium · $456,117 portfolio**.
- Recommendation counts: long-term contract **792** · 5% discount **663** · 15% discount **658** · welcome package **430** · online security **45** · autopay/check-in **18** · premium support **2**.
- Senior vs non-senior churn: **41.7% vs 23.6%**; no subgroup performance or offer-allocation audit has been run.

## Likely seminar questions and crisp answers

**Why Logistic Regression?**  
“It performed best on the benchmarked F1 and ROC-AUC in this experiment, and its coefficients make customer-level contributors easy to surface. LightGBM was close, so I chose the simpler, more interpretable model rather than claiming a dramatic performance win.”

**Why isn’t accuracy enough?**  
“Because 73.46% of this dataset stayed. Predicting everyone stays already gives 73.46% accuracy while catching no churners. I therefore report precision, recall, F1, and ROC-AUC too.”

**Is the test set truly untouched?**  
“It was held out from fitting, but I used it to compare and select the candidate models. So it is internal evaluation, not an untouched final test. A separate external cohort or nested validation is a next step.”

**Why is recall only 56.95%?**  
“At the 0.50 cutoff the model catches 213 of 374 test churners. Lowering the cutoff to 0.35 raises recall to 71.7% and F1 to 62.98%, but reduces precision and increases the number of customers flagged. The business must decide whether that outreach volume is worth it.”

**Why not deploy 0.35 now?**  
“The test result is promising, but threshold selection depends on contact capacity, false-positive cost, and the value of an intervention. I shouldn’t change the production policy without that decision and a measured rollout.”

**What does ROC-AUC 84.91% mean?**  
“It measures how well the model ranks a randomly selected churner above a non-churner across thresholds. It is not the same as accuracy, and it doesn’t prove the probabilities are calibrated.”

**Are the risk-band rates a separate validation?**  
“No. They are observed rates across this full dataset after scoring it. They show increasing in-dataset risk ordering, not independent generalization.”

**How do the recommendations work?**  
“They are deterministic business rules using contract, service, tenure, monthly charges, and risk tier. The model predicts risk; rules map customer profiles to suggested actions. We have not measured campaign uplift with an experiment.”

**Does $92,539 mean the campaign can save that amount?**  
“No. It is the sum of current monthly charges in the high-risk segment. It is exposure, not expected recoverable revenue or savings. An A/B test would be needed to measure lift.”

**Is the system biased against senior customers?**  
“I can’t conclude that from churn rates alone. Senior-customer churn is higher in this dataset, and senior status is a feature, so subgroup error rates and offer allocation must be audited before any age-sensitive campaign.”

**Why not deep learning?**  
“This is a 7,043-row tabular dataset. I benchmarked four conventional models; Logistic Regression was competitive and easier to explain. I have no evidence that a more complex model would improve the outcome enough to justify the loss in simplicity.”

**What does the app do to protect reports?**  
“Reports are scoped to a visitor using a signed cookie in this prototype. That is not a substitute for enterprise identity, access control, retention policy, and a security review before handling real customer data.”

**What would you do next?**  
“First, validate on a fresh cohort or use nested evaluation. Then agree an operating threshold with the business, calibrate probabilities, audit subgroup performance and offer allocation, and run a controlled campaign experiment. Add drift monitoring and retraining only after defining what to monitor and who owns it.”

## Demo and delivery checklist

### Before the session

- Warm the Render app shortly before speaking; the free tier can cold-start.
- Prepare a small **synthetic or approved** CSV; never upload real personal customer data during a public seminar.
- Open the app and repository links, verify the QR codes, and have slide 17 screenshots ready as fallback.
- Keep the deck in slideshow mode and use the footer rail to jump if the audience asks to revisit a slide.
- Confirm the `.pbit` link works if you plan to show it.

### During the talk

- Don’t read every bullet. Use the charts as evidence and keep transitions short.
- Slow down on slides 3, 5, 10, 12, 14, 15, and 20; these carry the problem, methodological caveats, performance trade-off, and business limits.
- Say “on this dataset,” “internal test split,” “rule-based recommendation,” and “revenue exposure” where appropriate.
- If the live app is slow, don’t wait: use screenshots, explain the workflow, and move on.
- End at slide 22; leave silence for the first question.

### Delivery posture

Be confident about the engineering and honest about the evidence. The strongest seminar answer is not “the model solves churn”; it is “the prototype measures risk, makes its decision path inspectable, and exposes exactly what still needs validation before business impact can be claimed.”
